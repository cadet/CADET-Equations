General model assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
\item the fluid density and viscosity are constant in time and space (implies incompressibility);
\item the fluid flow is laminar;
\item the column is operated under constant conditions (e.g., temperature, flow rate);
\item the process is isothermal (i.e., there are no thermal effects);
\item diffusion does not depend on the concentration of the components, viscosity of the fluid, or pressure;
\item there is no solid phase in the column (and thus no adsorption);
\end{itemize}


Specific model assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
\item the fluid only flows in the radial direction of the column (i.e., there is no flow in the axial and angular direction);
\item the column is a hollow cylinder with inner radius $P^{\mathrm{inner}}$ and outer radius $P^{\mathrm{outer}}$;
\item the column is radially symmetric and homogeneous (i.e., concentration profiles and parameters only depend on the axial position);
\end{itemize}


\section*{Radial Plug Flow}
Consider a hollow cylindrical column of axial bed height $H^{\mathrm{b}} > 0$ with inner radius $P^{\mathrm{inner}} > 0$ and outer radius $P^{\mathrm{outer}} > P^{\mathrm{inner}}$ filled with a liquid phase, and observed over a time interval $(0, T^{\mathrm{end}})$.
In the interstitial volume, mass transfer is governed by the following convection equations in $(0, T^{\mathrm{end}})\times (P^{\mathrm{inner}}, P^{\mathrm{outer}})$ and for all components $i\in\{1, \dots, N^{\mathrm{c}} \}$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i}}{\partial t} = - \frac{v}{\rho} \frac{\partial c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i}}{\partial \rho},
\end{align}
with boundary conditions

\begin{alignat}{2}
v c^{\mathrm{in}}_{i} &= \left.\left( v c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i} \right)\right|_{\rho=P^{\mathrm{inner}}} & &\qquad\text{on }(0, T^{\mathrm{end}}).
\end{alignat}
Here, $t\in (0, T^{\mathrm{end}})$ is the time coordinate, $\rho\in (P^{\mathrm{inner}}, P^{\mathrm{outer}})$ is the radial coordinate, $c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i}\colon (0, T^{\mathrm{end}})\times (P^{\mathrm{inner}}, P^{\mathrm{outer}}) \mapsto \mathbb{R}$ is the bulk liquid concentration, $Q> 0$ is the volumetric flow rate, and $v:= \frac{Q}{2 \pi H^{\mathrm{b}}}$ is the velocity coefficient.
Consistent initial values for all solution variables (concentrations) are defined at $t = 0$.
This model is a particular instance of the unified mathematical framework introduced in Breuer et al. (2026), which provides a rigorous justification of its assumptions and a systematic derivation of its governing equations.
