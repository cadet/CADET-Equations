General model assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
\item the fluid density and viscosity are constant in time and space (implies incompressibility);
\item the fluid flow is laminar;
\item the column is operated under constant conditions (e.g., temperature, flow rate);
\item the process is isothermal (i.e., there are no thermal effects);
\item diffusion does not depend on the concentration of the components, viscosity of the fluid, or pressure;
\item there is no solid phase in the column (and thus no adsorption);
\end{itemize}


Specific model assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
\item the fluid only flows in the axial direction of the column (i.e., there is no flow in the radial and angular direction);
\item the column has a conical frustum shape with linearly varying radius;
\item the column is radially symmetric and homogeneous (i.e., concentration profiles and parameters only depend on the axial position);
\end{itemize}


\section*{Frustum Plug Flow}
Consider a conical frustum column of length $L^{\mathrm{b}} > 0$ with inlet radius $P^{\mathrm{0}} > 0$ and outlet radius $P^{\mathrm{L^{\mathrm{b}}}} > 0$ filled with a liquid phase, and observed over a time interval $(0, T^{\mathrm{end}})$.
In the interstitial volume, mass transfer is governed by the following convection equations in $(0, T^{\mathrm{end}})\times (0, L^{\mathrm{b}})$ and for all components $i\in\{1, \dots, N^{\mathrm{c}} \}$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i}}{\partial t} = - \frac{v}{\rho(z)^{2}} \frac{\partial c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i}}{\partial z},
\end{align}
with boundary conditions

\begin{alignat}{2}
\frac{v}{\rho(z)^{2}} c^{\mathrm{in}}_{i} &= \left.\left( \frac{v}{\rho(z)^{2}} c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i} \right)\right|_{z=0} & &\qquad\text{on }(0, T^{\mathrm{end}}).
\end{alignat}
Here, $t\in (0, T^{\mathrm{end}})$ is the time coordinate, $z\in (0, L^{\mathrm{b}})$ is the axial frustum coordinate, $c^{\mathrm{b}}_{i}\colon (0, T^{\mathrm{end}})\times (0, L^{\mathrm{b}}) \mapsto \mathbb{R}$ is the bulk liquid concentration, $Q> 0$ is the volumetric flow rate, $\rho(z) = P^{\mathrm{0}} + \frac{P^{\mathrm{L^{\mathrm{b}}}} - P^{\mathrm{0}}}{L^{\mathrm{b}}} z$ is the column radius function, and $v:= \frac{Q}{\pi}$ is the velocity coefficient.
Consistent initial values for all solution variables (concentrations) are defined at $t = 0$.
This model is a particular instance of the unified mathematical framework introduced in Breuer et al. (2026), which provides a rigorous justification of its assumptions and a systematic derivation of its governing equations.
